# SMART Goals Framework

## Overview

The SMART framework is a widely used method for defining clear, actionable, and achievable goals. By ensuring a goal meets each SMART criterion, you increase the likelihood of success while making progress easier to track and evaluate.

SMART stands for:

- **Specific**
- **Measurable**
- **Achievable**
- **Relevant**
- **Time-Bound**

---

## Specific

A goal should clearly define what you want to accomplish.

Ask yourself:

- What exactly am I trying to achieve?
- Why is this important?
- Who is involved?
- What are the desired outcomes?

### Example

❌ _Improve my technical skills._

✅ _Learn the fundamentals of machine learning using Python and complete three hands-on projects._

---

## Measurable

A goal should include criteria that allow you to track progress and determine when it has been completed.

Ask yourself:

- How will I measure success?
- How will I know when the goal is complete?
- What metrics or milestones can I use?

### Example

❌ _Read more books._

✅ _Read 12 books this year, averaging one book per month._

---

## Achievable

A goal should be realistic given your available time, resources, skills, and constraints.

Ask yourself:

- Is this goal realistic?
- Do I have the necessary resources?
- What obstacles might prevent success?

### Example

❌ _Become an expert software engineer in one month._

✅ _Complete a Go programming course and build a small application within the next three months._

---

## Relevant

A goal should align with your broader objectives, priorities, and values.

Ask yourself:

- Why does this goal matter?
- Does it support my long-term goals?
- Is it worth investing time and effort?

### Example

❌ _Learn a technology unrelated to my career or interests without a clear purpose._

✅ _Learn cloud-native development to support upcoming work projects and career growth._

---

## Time-Bound

A goal should have a target date or deadline that creates focus and urgency.

Ask yourself:

- When should this be completed?
- What milestones should be reached along the way?
- What happens if I miss the deadline?

### Example

❌ _Someday I want to get certified._

✅ _Earn the AWS Certified Developer certification by March 31, 2027._

---

## SMART Goal Template

Use the following template when defining new goals:

**Goal Statement:**

> I will **[specific outcome]** by **[target date]**, measured by **[metrics or milestones]**, because it supports **[larger objective or area of responsibility]**.

### Example

> I will complete an online course and build three portfolio projects by June 30, 2027, measured by course completion and project delivery, because it supports my goal of growing skills in my chosen field.

---

## Common Mistakes

- Goals that are too vague.
- Lack of measurable outcomes.
- Unrealistic expectations.
- Goals that do not align with priorities.
- Missing deadlines.
- Focusing on activities rather than outcomes.

---

## Relationship to PARA

Within the [[PARA]] framework:

- **Projects** should generally be defined as SMART goals.
- **Areas** represent ongoing responsibilities and typically do not have SMART-defined end dates.
- **Resources** can contain supporting materials needed to achieve SMART goals.
- **Archives** store completed goals and projects for future reference.

A good rule of thumb:

> If it has a clear outcome and deadline, it is probably a **Project**. If it requires ongoing maintenance without a defined end date, it is probably an **Area**.
