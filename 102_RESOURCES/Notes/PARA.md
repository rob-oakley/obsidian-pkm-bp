# Organizing Knowledge and Work with the PARA Method

## Projects

Projects are activities with a clearly defined beginning, middle, and end. They are created to achieve a specific outcome and are considered complete when that outcome has been reached.

### Project Guidelines

Projects should follow the [[SMART Goals|SMART goal]] framework:

- **Specific**: Clearly define the desired outcome.
- **Measurable**: Establish criteria to track progress and success.
- **Achievable**: Ensure the goal is realistic given available resources.
- **Relevant**: Align the project with broader objectives and priorities.
- **Time-Bound**: Set a target completion date.

Additional project characteristics:

- Every project should have a defined deadline or target completion date.
- Projects should be actionable and outcome-oriented.
- Completed projects should be moved to the Archive.

---

## Areas

Areas represent ongoing responsibilities or interests that require continuous attention and maintenance. Unlike projects, areas do not have a defined end date.

### Types of Areas

#### Areas of Interest

Areas of Interest support learning, exploration, and personal growth. Examples include:

- Hobbies and recreational activities
- Learning a new skill or discipline
- Researching unfamiliar topics
- Exploring new ideas and concepts
- Developing new talents and capabilities

#### Areas of Responsibility

Areas of Responsibility represent long-term commitments that require consistent management. Examples include:

- Work and career development
- Personal life and relationships
- Health and wellness
- Financial management
- Home maintenance
- Education and professional development

### Key Characteristics of Areas

- Ongoing and continuous in nature
- Focused on maintaining standards rather than achieving a single outcome
- May generate projects when specific improvements or changes are needed
- Represent important parts of life that require regular attention

---

## Resources

Resources consist of reference materials, notes, research, and information that may be valuable in the future. They support projects and areas but are not themselves actionable commitments.

Examples of resources include:

- Study materials
- Research articles
- Documentation
- Bibliographies
- Meeting notes
- Training materials
- Reference guides

### Types of Notes

#### Fleeting Notes

Short-term notes intended to capture ideas before they are forgotten.

Examples:

- Quick observations
- Reminders
- Questions to investigate later
- Spontaneous ideas

#### Literature Notes

Notes created while consuming information from an external source.

Examples:

- Book highlights and summaries
- Podcast notes
- Training course notes
- Research article annotations
- Video summaries

#### Permanent Notes

Permanent notes capture your own understanding and insights derived from credible sources and experience.

Characteristics:

- Written in your own words
- Focused on a single idea or concept (atomic notes)
- Connected to related knowledge
- Designed for long-term retention and future use

### Maps of Content (MOCs)

Maps of Content (MOCs) are special index notes that organize and connect related information.

They serve as:

- Topic hubs
- Knowledge indexes
- Navigation tools for large collections of notes
- Mechanisms for discovering relationships between ideas

A well-maintained MOC helps transform a collection of disconnected notes into an interconnected knowledge network.

---

## Archives

Archives contain items that are no longer active but may still have historical or reference value.

Examples include:

- Completed projects
- Retired documentation
- Obsolete research
- Reference materials that are rarely used
- Notes that are no longer actively maintained

### Purpose of Archives

- Preserve knowledge without cluttering active workspaces.
- Maintain historical records for future reference.
- Store completed or inactive information in a centralized location.
- Reduce noise while ensuring important information remains accessible.

Think of the Archive as **cold storage for knowledge**: information that is not currently relevant to daily work but may become valuable again in the future.
